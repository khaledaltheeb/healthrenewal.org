Arabic Speech-Language Pathology & Audiology Review Pilot

Prepared by Health Renewal / Rawafid in Jordan for OPPHLA review consideration. The purpose is deliberately narrow: make a small set of Arabic public-education resources easy to inspect for terminology, professional boundaries, multilingual/cultural validity, referral safety, and material omissions before any external-review claim is made.

Status and attribution boundary. These resources are drafts for external professional review. Health Renewal / Rawafid does not claim OPPHLA endorsement, approval, accreditation, or co-authorship. OPPHLA's name or identity will not be presented as an endorsement unless OPPHLA explicitly authorizes the exact wording in writing.

How the resources were built

1 · Source-first authoring

Each resource is built from traceable professional or public-health sources, with primary reliance on sources such as ASHA Practice Portal and other authoritative guidance relevant to the topic.

2 · Arabic/RTL adaptation

The material is written for Arabic readers rather than mechanically translated. Terminology, sentence structure, right-to-left presentation, family readability, and the coexistence of Arabic varieties are considered explicitly.

3 · Scope & safety boundaries

Public education is kept separate from diagnosis and individualized treatment. Referral points and the roles of SLPs, audiologists, physicians, schools, and families are stated where relevant.

4 · Multilingual safeguards

Language or dialect difference is not treated as disorder. The draft resources explicitly warn against judging multilingual children from a single-language norm when that would be clinically inappropriate.

5 · Internal QA, then external review

Internal editorial review precedes external professional review. External review is documented only after it actually occurs, and reviewer feedback is used to produce a change log.

What would be most useful from OPPHLA

  • Terminology: wording that is inaccurate, unnatural, overly literal, or professionally misleading.
  • Safety and scope: any statement that crosses the boundary between public education and individualized assessment/treatment.
  • Referral boundaries: anything that should trigger SLP, audiology, medical, developmental, or school-team referral more clearly.
  • Multilingual/cultural validity: language that could misclassify dialectal or multilingual difference as disorder, or that needs cultural qualification.
  • Major omissions: clinically or professionally important points that a public Arabic resource should not omit.

A line-by-line copyedit is not necessary. High-value corrections and boundary issues are more useful to us than stylistic polishing.

Reciprocal value and possible collaboration

  • Arabic/RTL adaptation for OPPHLA: with permission, Health Renewal can adapt an eligible OPPHLA public-education resource into Arabic/RTL, preserve attribution and source traceability, and return the draft for OPPHLA approval before publication.
  • Arabic/MENA dissemination: where OPPHLA considers it appropriate, approved public resources can be made more accessible to Arabic-speaking families and professionals through Health Renewal's Arabic digital infrastructure.
  • Accessibility and digital presentation: we can assist with structured web presentation, accessible formatting, Arabic metadata, and terminology mapping for suitable public resources.
  • Bounded professional-review pathway: if this pilot is useful, future work can stay low-burden by using small, defined batches with transparent change logs rather than open-ended review requests.
  • Education and knowledge exchange: if OPPHLA has public educational initiatives suitable for international audiences, we are open to responsible Arabic knowledge-translation, resource exchange, or another mutually useful collaboration model.
Methodological principle. We prefer correction over endorsement. If a reviewer identifies a weakness, the expected outcome is a documented correction—not a request to approve wording that should be changed.
